# Illustrated learning journey

Use this optional authoring mode for a zoomable tutorial on one continuous canvas.
The overview should invite exploration; each closer view should teach something.
Adapt the topic, length, direction, and visual style to the audience and request.

## Prepare the lesson

Before drawing, outline the learning question, audience, prerequisite knowledge,
and conceptual progression. Start with the whole idea and its purpose, then
reveal mechanisms, choices, and limits. Research claims when needed and retain
sources for a compact reading area or accompanying notes.

Choose a recurring concrete example so new concepts build on a familiar situation.
Plan an opening scene with a situation, a change or problem, and a question that
the tutorial will eventually revisit. Let illustrations explain relationships or
mechanisms: a magnifier for examining a fact, a shared book for vocabulary, or a
source document for evidence. Avoid giving every concept the same box treatment.

## Establish the visual language before expanding

Use a supplied reference, or optionally generate a composition reference with
available image-generation tools. Study its visual hierarchy, spacing, palette,
illustrative motifs, and reading rhythm. A generated reference can contain wrong
text or relationships; verify the lesson independently.

For an editable infographic, reconstruct meaningful elements with native text,
paths, rectangles, circles, and connectors. Keep text editable and illustrations
composed of individually editable parts. Use raster art when requested or useful,
without substituting a flattened poster for requested editable work.

Build a representative stretch first: the opening, one or two complete concept
islands, a transition, and a small margin explanation. Inspect it at reading size
before multiplying the design. This tests storytelling and composition together.
If the user requests piece-by-piece review, finish the current agreed piece and
wait for their next instruction. Otherwise use these as internal checkpoints and
continue without introducing mandatory approval gates.

## Grow the journey island by island

Give each island one central teaching point, a meaningful illustration, concise
explanation, and a takeaway or natural transition. Reuse the established palette,
typography, recurring entities, and illustration conventions. Vary island size
and composition according to what the concept needs; avoid slide-like frames or
a uniform grid of text panels. Do not impose a fixed number of islands.

Reserve room for small teaching moments beside the main path. These can explain
an unfamiliar term, expose a hidden assumption, or show a practical limitation.
For example, a knowledge-graph lesson might use a fingerprint for identity, an
open book for ontology, and a document for provenance. Choose moments relevant
to the actual topic; they should enrich rather than interrupt the main story.

## Compose and connect

Plan approximate positions early, then refine the arrangement as islands take
shape. Use an overall downward progression by default, with gentle lateral
movement and enough space to read each section. Match a different requested
direction. Place transitions through open space and near the next learning stop.
Avoid long return loops, lines across text, or arrows that dominate the lesson.
Use reference-inspired curves when appropriate; numbering and spatial proximity
can also guide readers. Distinguish navigation from arrows expressing actual
relationships inside diagrams.

Do not shrink the whole document merely to force all text to be readable in one
viewport. A large tutorial needs zooming. Bulk resizing can clamp small shapes
to minimum dimensions and alter illustration proportions; inspect actual geometry
and rendered details after transformations.

End by returning to the opening question: what can the reader now explain, what
remains uncertain, and what could they try next? Keep conclusions proportional
to the evidence.

## Review at two scales

At overview scale, check the entry point, reading order, visual balance, spacing,
and ending. At reading scale, inspect text wrapping, illustration details, arrow
direction, overlaps, and the relationship between margin notes and their islands.
After bulk edits, verify retained objects and connector endpoints against fresh
board state. A successful edit response alone does not establish visual quality.

Finish with the shared Sabura save-and-verification workflow and leave the opening
or the revised area in a useful view. Distinguish an export request from confirmed
file delivery.
